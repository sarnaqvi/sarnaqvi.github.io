01 — Bio

Sarah Naqvi is a musician, composer and researcher working at the seam where acoustics meets emotion.

Trained in both physics and composition, Sarah builds instruments that translate scientific datasets into music — glacial melt into slow strings, neural activity into rhythm. The work has been performed in planetariums, printed in journals, and pressed to vinyl.

Based between a laboratory and a listening room, the practice refuses the line between the analytical and the felt. Every piece begins as a question.

  • Focus Data sonification & psychoacoustics
  • Based in Reykjavík / Berlin
  • Since 2014

Notes & manifestos

01

Why a glacier should be heard, not just measured

Numbers describe a retreating ice sheet with precision and no urgency. Translate that same dataset into a falling melody and the room goes quiet. This is an argument for sonification as a tool of both science and conscience.

02

Psychoacoustics: the instrument is your skull

Before a sound is music it is a pressure wave bending through bone and fluid. Understanding the mechanics of hearing changes how I compose — I write for the ear's blind spots as much as its sensitivities.

On building instruments that don't exist yet

Every dataset demands a new instrument. A field note on prototyping software synthesizers that map neural activity to rhythm, and why constraints from physics make better music than freedom does.
